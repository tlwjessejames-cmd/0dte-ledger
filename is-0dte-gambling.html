Is 0DTE gambling?

It can be, and often is. The honest answer depends less on the instrument than on the structure around it. This page sets out what makes a 0DTE trade gambling-like, and what a graded public experiment does differently.

The short answer

A 0DTE option is a contract that expires the same day it is traded. Nothing about that fact alone makes a trade a gamble or an investment. The same contract can be either, depending on whether the person holding it has a rule for entering, a definition of success fixed in advance, and a record they cannot quietly edit afterwards. Without those three things, a 0DTE trade is a bet on a price moving far enough, fast enough, before a clock runs out. That is a fair description of gambling, whatever the ticker is.

What makes 0DTE gambling-like

The expiry is hours away. A normal option position can be wrong for days and still recover. A 0DTE contract has no such margin. Time decay is not a slow background cost; it completes inside a single session. A read that is eventually right but late is worth exactly the same as a read that was simply wrong.

Total loss is a routine outcome. If the underlying does not reach the strike, the contract expires worthless and the whole premium is gone. This is not a tail risk to be managed. It is one of the two ordinary endings of the trade, and it happens to experienced and inexperienced holders alike.

The leverage is extreme. A small premium controls the price movement of a much larger notional position. Returns of several hundred percent in a session are possible, and so are losses of one hundred percent in the same session. Instruments that pay out like that attract betting behaviour even from people who did not intend to bet.

The shape feels binary. Win or lose, settled by the close, decided the same day it was placed. Short feedback loops are what make gambling products repeatable at high frequency, and 0DTE has the shortest feedback loop in listed markets.

What a graded experiment does differently

The 0DTE Ledger is a paper experiment that began on Thursday, 22 October 2026. It places no orders and risks no money. Each market weekday, one 0DTE QQQ pick is posted before the open and graded the same day. What separates that from gambling is not the contract. It is the apparatus around the contract.

The rules are written before the fact and frozen. One pick per day. One contract, one target, stated before the 06:30 PT open. No alternates, no second chances, no changing the question after the answer is known. The rules change only at scheduled reviews; the next is Thursday, 17 December 2026, and a review may propose changes but applies none by itself.

Success is defined in advance. A day is a win if QQQ touches the stated target intraday in the regular session, and a loss otherwise. Prints outside the regular session do not grade. Graded returns are measured off the contract’s opening print, a price fixed by the market at the open, not chosen afterwards.

The losses are published at the same size as the wins. Every graded day stays in the record, in the same format, and graded rows are never edited. A gambling habit survives by forgetting its losses or retelling them as near misses. A ledger cannot do either.

What the record does and does not show

After seven graded days the record stands at 5 wins and 2 losses, a win rate of 71.4 percent, an average max return of +132.4 percent, and an average expiry return of +36.6 percent. Those figures describe seven days. Seven days is a tiny sample, and a win rate computed on it is a description of those days only. It is not evidence that the pick process has an edge, and it is not a forecast of any future day.

The record also contains the kind of day that keeps the accounting honest: a day graded as a win, because QQQ touched the target, where holding the contract to expiry returned -5.2 percent. Both statements are printed, because the grade answers one question and the expiry return answers another.

None of this proves that trading 0DTE options with real money, under real loss, would resemble the paper record. It proves something narrower: that a daily pick can be stated in advance, graded by a fixed rule, and published without selection. Whether that is worth anything is what the December review, and a much larger sample, are for.
